  How to use this + what's wrong
  v04's overall cut rate already matches the references (1.3s average shot). What it lacks is
  the hook energy, the type styling and the motion graphics, plus five dead zones where the
  frame barely moves. Fix those and it reads like the references.

  (Hook), then Section 4 (Timestamped changes), then Section 5 (QA). Every timestamp
  refers to RC_GTA-Origin_v04_preview.mp4 (77.6s, 1280x720, 30fps). Each item also
  quotes the on-screen caption, so it can be found even after earlier edits shift the timing.
  Apply Section 4 from the end of the video backwards, so earlier changes don't move later
  timestamps.

  Note: I read the voice-over from your on-screen captions, not from the audio, since there
  was no speech-to-text in this workspace. If a caption differs from what's actually said, go
  with the audio.

  What the references do that v04 doesn't
          Words animate in with colour. In Trailer 1 and 2, every word enters in an accent colour
          (pink-red), slightly blurred and scaled up, then settles to white within about 4 frames.
          v04's words hard-pop in, already white, and then sit still.
          Type contrast. The references mix a small upright sans for setup words with a big
          display face for the key word, often an elegant serif (Didot-style: "Noah Politis",
          "Marcus Ryu", years). v04 uses one italic face for everything.
          Hero words are huge and sit behind the subject. "podcast", "today's guest", "killed"
          and "Failure" fill most of the frame and are masked behind people. v04's biggest
          words are about 14% of frame height and always sit on top.
          Highlight pills and physical props. Cluely uses neon pill boxes with type-on text, a
          rubber stamp slamming "SCAMMED", tilted paper documents, and an ERROR window.
          v04 has almost none of this.
          Archival media sits on designed backgrounds. The references show inserts as tilted
          cards with borders and shadows on textured backgrounds. v04 drops 4:3 archival on
          flat black.

  Layout rule: the setup line sits directly above the key word, left-aligned to it, with the key
  word 3.2–3.8x the setup size. That's the stacked "small over big" look of both trailers.


  2.3 Word entry animation (apply to every caption word)
  Right now words appear in one frame, already white, and don't move. Replace that with
  the trailer-style entry:

      1. Frame 0: word at opacity 0%, scale 112%, Gaussian blur 6px, colour #FF2E7E .
      2. Frames 0–2: opacity to 100%.
      3. Frames 0–4: scale 112%→100% and blur 6→0px, ease-out (cubic-bezier 0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1).
      4. Frames 2–6: colour cross-fades from #FF2E7E to white. Key words (tier B) hold pink
         until frame 4, then fade by frame 8.
      5. For the whole life of the caption block: slow drift scale 100%→104%, so the text is
          never fully static.
      6. Exit: cut with the shot. If the text has to leave before the cut, use a 3-frame blur-out
          (0→8px) plus opacity to 0. No slow fades.
      7. Time each word's frame 0 to the VO syllable onset, never early. A word appearing 1–2
          frames after the sound feels punchier than one that arrives before it.

  2.4 Motion-graphics kit (build once, reuse)
          Highlight pill (Cluely-style): rounded rectangle, 10px radius, fill #FF2E7E at 92%, 3px
          white inner stroke inset 6px, outer glow 24px at 40%. Text: Montserrat ExtraBold,
          uppercase, white, tracking +2%, 44–52px. Animate the box width 0→100% from the
          left over 6 frames, then type the text on at about 30 characters per second. Exit with
          the pixel-glitch transition.
          Pixel-glitch transition (pink): 6–8 frames. Blocky rectangles of #FF2E7E and white
          tear horizontally across the frame (datamosh/pixel-sort), with RGB-split 6px on
          frames 2–5. Cluely uses this between beats at about 7.0s, 13.5s, 31.0s and 57.0s.
          Rubber stamp: a red #E3262B grunge stamp with a rough edge, rotated -12°. Animate
          scale 180%→95%→100% over 5 frames, then a 1-frame white flash and a 4px camera
          shake for 4 frames. Optional: a 3D wooden stamp handle that lifts away, as Cluely does
          at 1.9–2.4s. Paper-thud SFX.
          Win95 error window: recreate the "This program has performed an illegal operation
          and will be shut down." dialog (the one already visible on the CRT at 8.0s) as a crisp,
          full-size graphic so it can be scaled big and duplicated.
          Card insert: for any 4:3 or archival clip. White 10px border, 2–4° tilt, drop shadow (0,
          18px, 40px blur, 50%), on a background of the same clip scaled 130%, blurred 40px
          and darkened 50%. Or use an off-white paper texture (Trailer 1 style). Never on flat
          black.
          Highlighter sweep: #FF2E7E at 55% with multiply blend. Wipes left→right across the
          line over 8 frames, with a 100%→110% push-in on the document underneath.
          Hand-drawn annotation: your yellow marker arrow, circle or underline, written on over
          8–10 frames (stroke trim path), slight jitter.
          Flash-star burst (Trailer 1): a white 4-point star flare that pops at a cut point for 4–6
          frames. Use it on 2–3 big reveals only.


  2.5 Depth masking (text behind subject)
  Rotoscope the subject (a person, car or hill) and place the hero word on a layer between
  the background and the subject, so part of the word hides behind them. Trailer 1 does this
  with "podcast" (1.75–2.25s) and Trailer 2 with "today's guest" (4.0s). Use it at the
  timestamps flagged [DEPTH] in Sections 3 and 4.

  2.6 Texture and grade
          Keep the dark vignette. Add a global film grain at 3–5% (monochrome, size 1.2) so
          gameplay, archival and black cards feel like one piece.
          Archival 1995 footage: add 1–2px chromatic aberration and a light VHS line wobble.
          Keep modern GTA V/VI gameplay clean, so the eras read differently.
          Black text cards: replace pure black with near-black #0E0E10 plus grain, and put a
          darkened, blurred loop of relevant footage behind at 15–25% opacity. The frame
          should never be dead black for more than 0.5s (see Section 4).


  2.7 Sound design (the references are dense with it)
          Every word pop: a soft tick or click at -24 dB (vary between 3 samples so it doesn't
          sound mechanical).
          Every key word: a short whoosh into it plus a low thump at -14 dB.
          Every hard cut in the hook: a whoosh or swipe.
          Specific moments: police siren sweep (0:00, 0:03.6), keyboard clatter under the CRT
          (4.5–6.4s), the Windows 95 "critical stop" chord (7.5s), glitch stutters (8.8s, 32.0s),
          stamp thud (30.4s), a reverse-cymbal riser into each big reveal (31.5s, 53.0s, 1:15.5),
          and a bass drop or impact on "Grand Theft Auto" (53.5s).
          Music: duck the bed by 6–8 dB under VO. Cut the music to silence for 2–6 frames right
          before "broke.", "breaks." and the GTA logo, then hit on the word. That's a micro-
          silence, not 1.6s (see 54.5s).
          Final mix: keep integrated loudness at -14 LUFS, true peak -1 dBTP (you're already at
          -14.0).

  Pacing rules (paste with the rest)
      1. 0–4.5s: a full-frame visual change every 0.4–0.7s. No inset on black, no build-ups that
         leave empty frame.
      2. 4.5–12s: no shot longer than 1.2s.
      3. Rest of video: no shot longer than 2.0s. If a shot must hold longer (an interview or
         document), give it a push-in of at least 8% plus a new text or graphic event every 0.7s
         or less.

         behind.
